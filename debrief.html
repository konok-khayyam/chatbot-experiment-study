Thank You

Thank you for taking part in this study.

This study examines how different chatbot interaction styles influence people's responses to manipulative or extremist-style online content.

In particular, we are interested in whether different ways of interacting with a chatbot can support more reflective engagement, openness to alternative perspectives, and resistance to the influence of manipulative online messages.

About the Study Materials

The video you watched was fictional and created for this study. It was designed to simulate a one-sided persuasive message that people might encounter in an online environment.

The views and claims presented in the video do not represent or endorse the views of the researchers, TU Wien, Curtin University, or the institutions involved in this study.

Some manipulative or extremist-style online messages can use themes such as grievance, identity threat, urgency, dehumanisation, or claims that harmful action is necessary or justified. The study uses fictional or sanitised material to examine how people respond to this type of communication.

Chatbot Interaction Conditions

Participants were randomly assigned to different versions of the chatbot interaction as part of the experimental design.

These versions differed in aspects of friendliness, conversational strategy, and empathy. The different chatbot approaches were designed to examine how conversational design may influence people's reflection on and responses to the content they had viewed.

The chatbot was a research prototype used only for this study. The purpose of the interaction was not to endorse the claims presented in the video, but to examine how different conversational approaches affect participants' engagement with those claims.

Support and Withdrawal

Taking part in this study may have raised difficult thoughts or feelings for some people. If anything came up for you, support is available.

Participation was voluntary, and you could stop participating if you felt uncomfortable.

If you have questions about the study or wish to request withdrawal of your data, please contact the research team using the information below.
